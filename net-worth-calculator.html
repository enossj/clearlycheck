What net worth actually measures

Net worth is the single clearest snapshot of your financial health. The formula is short — net worth = total assets − total liabilities — but it captures something income alone can't: not how much you earn, but how much you've actually kept and built. Two people earning the same salary can have wildly different net worth depending on debt, saving, and investing habits.

How to value your assets

Always use current market value, not what you originally paid. Look up your home's estimated value, use the trade-in or private-sale price for vehicles, and take the latest balances from your bank, brokerage, and retirement statements. Be honest and slightly conservative — inflating asset values just gives you a number that isn't real. Small everyday possessions are usually left out because their resale value is negligible and they're not worth the effort to track.

Don't forget the liabilities

Liabilities are everything you owe: the outstanding balance on your mortgage, car loans, student loans, credit cards, and any personal, medical, or tax debt. Use the current payoff balance, not the original loan amount. It's the side people most often underestimate, and leaving debts out is the fastest way to get a net worth number that looks better than reality.

Reading the result

A positive net worth means your assets outweigh your debts. A negative net worth — common for younger people with student loans or a fresh mortgage — means the opposite, and it's usually temporary. The single number matters less than its direction over time. Recalculate every six or twelve months; a line that trends upward as you pay down debt and grow savings is the real goal, regardless of where you start.

Ways to grow your net worth

  • Pay down high-interest debt first. Every dollar of credit-card balance you clear raises net worth immediately and stops the interest drain — see our debt free calculator.
  • Automate saving and investing. Steady contributions let compound interest do the heavy lifting over decades.
  • Build an emergency fund. It keeps a surprise expense from becoming new high-interest debt.
  • Track it, don't obsess over it. Check a couple of times a year and focus on the trend.

Sources & method

This calculator uses the standard accounting identity net worth = total assets − total liabilities, the same basis used in a personal balance sheet and by financial institutions and regulators such as the US Federal Reserve in its Survey of Consumer Finances. All figures stay in your browser and are never transmitted.
