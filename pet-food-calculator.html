How pet calorie needs are calculated

Vets size a pet's food to its energy needs in two steps. First the resting energy requirement (RER) — the calories needed at rest — using the formula that works across all sizes:

RER = 70 × (body weight in kg)0.75

Then RER is multiplied by a life-stage factor to get the maintenance energy requirement (MER) — the calories for a normal day. A couch-loving neutered adult sits around 1.2–1.6× RER, while a growing puppy or a working dog can be 2–3× or more. This calculator does both steps and lets you pick the factor that matches your pet.

Turning calories into cups

Calories are the accurate part; cups are where people go wrong. Energy density varies enormously between foods — anywhere from about 300 to over 500 kcal per cup — so a generic "feed 2 cups" chart on the bag can be very wrong for your specific food. Always divide your pet's daily calorie target by the kcal per cup printed on your bag (or kcal per can for wet food), then split the total across meals. Enter that number above and the tool shows the amount for you.

Don't forget treats and the 10% rule

Treats count. A good guideline is to keep treats to no more than 10% of daily calories and reduce meal portions to make room for them, or they quietly cause weight gain. Table scraps, dental chews, and training treats all add up faster than most owners expect.

Watch body condition, not just the number

Use the calculated amount as a starting point, then let your pet's body tell you the rest. You should be able to feel the ribs easily and see a waist from above. Re-check every couple of weeks and adjust up or down by 5–10%. For any weight-loss program, a puppy or kitten, pregnancy, or a medical condition, plan the diet with your veterinarian.

Sources & method

This tool uses the standard RER = 70 × (kg)0.75 formula and life-stage multipliers published by veterinary nutrition sources including the World Small Animal Veterinary Association (WSAVA) global nutrition guidelines, the National Research Council (NRC) companion-animal energy requirements, and the Association of American Feed Control Officials (AAFCO) feeding framework. Results are estimates for healthy adult pets; individual needs vary widely. This is general guidance, not veterinary advice — confirm your pet's diet, especially for weight loss, growth, or medical conditions, with your veterinarian.
