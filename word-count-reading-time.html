What this tool counts

As you type or paste, this counter updates six figures in real time: words, characters (with and without spaces), sentences, paragraphs, and estimated reading and speaking time. Everything happens locally in your browser using JavaScript, so your text is never uploaded, stored, or seen by anyone — safe for drafts, private notes, or confidential work.

How reading time is estimated

Reading time is simply your word count divided by a reading speed in words per minute (wpm). Research on adult silent reading of everyday prose puts the average at roughly 200 to 250 wpm. This tool lets you switch between three speeds so the estimate fits your audience:

  • Slow (100 wpm) — dense, technical, or unfamiliar material, or non-native readers.
  • Average (200 wpm) — typical adult reading a blog post, article, or email.
  • Fast (300 wpm) — confident readers skimming familiar content.

Reading time vs. speaking time

Reading silently is much faster than reading aloud. A comfortable speaking pace is around 130 words per minute, which is why this tool shows speaking time separately — useful for speeches, presentations, voiceovers, and video scripts. As a rule of thumb:

Words or characters — which counts?

It depends on the platform. Essays, articles, and books are measured in words; social posts and meta descriptions are measured in characters. A few common limits: an SEO meta description is best kept near 150-160 characters, a tweet is 280 characters, and a college essay is usually set in words. This tool shows both so you can hit whichever limit applies.

Tips for hitting a target length

  • Over the limit? Cut filler words (very, really, just, actually), merge short sentences, and remove repetition.
  • Under the limit? Add a concrete example, expand a claim with evidence, or address a counterpoint — padding with filler is obvious to readers.
  • Writing a speech? Aim for the speaking-time figure, not reading time, and rehearse aloud to check the real pace.

Sources & method

Word counts split text on whitespace; sentences are counted by terminal punctuation (. ! ?) and paragraphs by line breaks, so unusual formatting can shift the sentence and paragraph totals slightly. Reading-speed ranges (about 200-250 wpm for silent reading, ~130 wpm for speaking) reflect widely reported averages from reading research; individual speed varies with material and reader. Reading and speaking times are estimates for planning, not exact measurements. All processing happens in your browser.
