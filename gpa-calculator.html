How GPA is calculated

GPA is a credit-weighted average, not a simple average of your grades. Each course's grade is converted to grade points, multiplied by that course's credit hours, and all of these "quality points" are added together. That total is then divided by your total credit hours:

GPA = (Σ grade points × credit hours) ÷ (Σ credit hours)

This is why a 4-credit A has four times the impact on your GPA that a 1-credit A does — credit hours act as a weight, not just a label.

Pass/No Pass and ungraded courses

Courses graded Pass (P) or No Pass (NP) — along with any row left at the default "–" — are excluded from GPA entirely, not counted as zero. That's the standard convention: pass/fail grading exists specifically so a course doesn't affect your GPA either way, even though it still earns credit toward your degree. This calculator leaves them out of both the quality-points total and the credit-hours total used to compute your average, and shows "—" in the breakdown table instead of a misleading 0.00.

Weighted vs. unweighted GPA

An unweighted GPA treats every class the same — an A in gym counts identically to an A in AP Calculus, capped at 4.0. A weighted GPA adds bonus points for harder courses to reflect the extra difficulty, commonly +0.5 for Honors and +1.0 for AP, IB, or college-level courses. This is why some students report weighted GPAs above 4.0. Exact bonus amounts vary significantly by school and even by district, so treat this as a planning estimate rather than your official number.

Cumulative vs. semester GPA

Your semester GPA reflects only the courses you're currently entering. Your cumulative GPA — the number that typically matters for transcripts, scholarships, and applications — combines every semester you've completed. To calculate it accurately, you need both your previous GPA and the total credit hours it was based on, since a 3.5 GPA built on 15 credits carries much less weight than one built on 90 credits when averaging in a new semester.
