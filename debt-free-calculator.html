How Does the Debt Payoff Calculator Work?

This debt payoff calculator estimates how long it can take to repay one or more debts using the balance, APR, and minimum monthly payment you enter. For multiple debts, you can compare minimum payments with the debt avalanche and debt snowball methods.

Monthly interest = Current balance × (APR ÷ 12)
Principal paid = Monthly payment − Monthly interest
Payoff time = Number of monthly payments until balance reaches $0

The calculator uses a month-by-month amortization simulation. Your actual payoff date can differ if your lender changes the interest rate, charges fees, applies payments differently, or you change the amount you pay.

How Long Will It Take to Pay Off My Debt?

Your debt payoff date depends mainly on your balance, interest rate, and monthly payment. With multiple debts, the way you allocate payments can also change the timeline and total interest.

Use the calculator to see your estimated debt-free date, payoff time in months and years, total interest, and total amount paid. You can also add an extra monthly payment to see how paying more could change the result.

How Extra Payments Can Help You Pay Off Debt Faster

An extra payment reduces the principal sooner, which can reduce the interest charged in future months. Enter an amount in the extra monthly payment field to compare the selected payoff plan with the minimum-payment baseline.

The results show months saved and interest saved when the calculation supports a meaningful comparison.

Debt Avalanche vs. Debt Snowball

The debt avalanche method directs extra money toward the debt with the highest APR first. The debt snowball method directs extra money toward the smallest outstanding balance first. Both approaches keep minimum payments going on the other active debts.

  • Debt avalanche: prioritize the highest-interest debt first. This focuses extra payments where the interest rate is highest.
  • Debt snowball: prioritize the smallest balance first. When one debt is cleared, its payment capacity can be rolled into the next target.
  • Minimum payments: pay the required amount on each debt without intentionally redirecting freed payments to another debt.

There is no single payoff strategy that fits every situation. The calculator lets you compare the timelines and interest costs produced by each method using the numbers you enter.

Credit Card Debt Payoff

You can use this as a credit card payoff calculator by entering each card as a separate debt. Add the current balance, APR, and minimum payment for each card, then compare the credit card debt payoff timeline under minimum payments, avalanche, or snowball.

This can also work for personal loans, auto loans, student loans, and other debts when you know the balance, interest rate, and payment amount.

Understanding APR and Monthly Interest

Your APR is the annual percentage rate used to describe the cost of borrowing. For this calculator's monthly estimate, the annual rate is divided by 12 to estimate the monthly interest rate.

For example, an 18% APR corresponds to a 1.5% monthly rate in this simplified calculation. On a $10,000 balance, that would be $150 of interest for one month before considering the payment.

How to Pay Off Debt Faster

  • Increase your monthly payment when your budget allows.
  • Use the debt avalanche if you want extra payments directed toward the highest APR first.
  • Use the debt snowball if you prefer to target the smallest balance first.
  • Avoid adding new high-interest debt while you are paying existing balances down.
  • Check your lender's terms before using balance transfers, consolidation, or refinancing, because fees and promotional rates can change the overall cost.

What Happens If My Payment Barely Covers the Interest?

If a payment is less than or equal to the interest that accrues during a month, the balance may not decrease. The calculator flags debts where the entered minimum payment is not enough to cover the estimated monthly interest.

Is My Data Private?

Yes. This calculator runs in your browser using JavaScript. The debt amounts and payment information you enter are not sent to a ClearlyCheck server by the calculator.
