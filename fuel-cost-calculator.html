How trip fuel cost is calculated

This calculator works backward from your vehicle's real-world fuel economy. First it figures out how much fuel your trip actually uses: divide the distance by your fuel economy in MPG, or multiply the distance by your economy in L/100km and divide by 100. That fuel amount, in gallons or liters, is then multiplied by the price you enter to get your total fuel cost. If you check the round-trip box, the distance is doubled before anything else is calculated, since a round trip means driving the route twice.

MPG vs. L/100km: which is better?

MPG (miles per gallon) and L/100km (liters per 100 kilometers) measure the same thing — how far a vehicle goes on a given amount of fuel — but they run in opposite directions. With MPG, a higher number means better fuel economy, since you're going further on each gallon. With L/100km, a lower number is better, since you're using less fuel to cover the same distance. That inverse relationship trips a lot of people up when comparing a US-spec car's MPG rating to a European-spec car's L/100km rating, so this calculator lets you enter either one directly without needing to convert by hand first.

Tips for cutting your trip's fuel cost

A few habits make a real dent in what you spend at the pump over a long trip:

  • Keep tires properly inflated. Underinflated tires increase rolling resistance and can lower fuel economy by several percent.
  • Drive a steady speed. Fuel economy drops sharply above roughly 60 mph, and aggressive acceleration and braking waste fuel that a steady pace doesn't.
  • Cut excess weight and drag. Roof boxes, bike racks, and a trunk full of unused gear all add drag or weight that increase how much fuel your engine burns.
  • Shop around for price. Gas prices can vary by 20–30 cents a gallon between stations a few miles apart, especially near highway exits versus a mile or two off the interstate.

Sources & method

Fuel used is calculated with the standard formula: fuel = distance ÷ fuel economy (for MPG), or fuel = distance × (economy ÷ 100) (for L/100km). Total cost is fuel used multiplied by the price you enter. This calculator doesn't pull live fuel prices from any feed — enter the price you see at the pump or expect to pay, since prices vary by location, station, and day. Check a source like GasBuddy or AAA's fuel price tracker for current regional averages if you don't know your trip's expected price.
