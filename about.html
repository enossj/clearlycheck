Why I built this

I'm learning data analytics with finance as my target field — working through spreadsheets, SQL, Python, and a lot of trial and error. Along the way, I kept needing quick answers to everyday money questions: how long until this debt is paid off, whether renting or buying makes more sense, what a salary actually works out to per hour.

Every time, the calculators I found online were paywalled behind a "premium" tier, buried under a wall of ads before you could see a result, or gave an answer with no explanation of how it got there. I figured if I was frustrated by that, other people probably were too — so I started building the tools I wished existed: free, fast, and honest about how the number was calculated.

Who's actually behind this

Just me. There's no team, no company registration, no outside funding — I design, build, and write every tool on this site myself. I'm saying this plainly because I'd rather you know exactly what ClearlyCheck is than have it pretend to be bigger than it is. If that changes down the line, I'll update this page.

How I try to keep things accurate

ClearlyCheck's tools live in a category — personal finance and health calculators — where getting a number wrong can actually affect someone's decisions. I take that seriously, even as a one-person site. In practice, that means:

  • Figures come from primary sources where they exist — IRS revenue procedures for tax brackets, official CDC/WHO guidance for health formulas, not secondhand blog posts.
  • Calculations are checked against known worked examples before publishing, not just assumed correct because the formula looks right.
  • Every tool explains its own limitations — what it does and doesn't cover — instead of presenting an estimate as a guarantee.
  • Nothing here replaces a professional. These tools are for getting a fast, informed starting point — not for replacing an accountant, financial advisor, or doctor.
