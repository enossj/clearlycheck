The Illusion of the Gig Economy "Hourly Rate"

When ride-share and delivery platforms advertise earning potentials like "$25 an hour," they are almost exclusively referring to Gross Earnings. However, as an independent contractor (1099 worker), you are essentially operating a micro-business. Your gross revenue is entirely different from your net profit.

Unlike W-2 employees, gig workers are entirely responsible for their own overhead. Every mile driven depreciates your asset, consumes fuel, and requires maintenance. Furthermore, independent contractors are responsible for both the employee and employer portions of Medicare and Social Security, known collectively as the Self-Employment (SE) Tax.

1. Tracking Active Miles vs. Deadhead Miles

One of the most common mistakes new drivers make is only tracking their "active" miles (the miles driven while a passenger or delivery is in the car). For accurate profitability and tax write-offs, you must track your total mileage from the moment you turn the app on to the moment you return home.

  • Active Miles: Miles driven directly for a fare.
  • Deadhead Miles: Miles driven between fares, repositioning to a busy zone, or returning home after a shift.

Deadhead miles cost you just as much in gas and depreciation, and they are fully deductible against your gross income.

2. Understanding the IRS Standard Mileage Deduction

To simplify vehicle expense tracking, the IRS provides a standard mileage rate. For the second half of 2026, this rate is 76 cents per mile. This single rate bundles the cost of:

  • Gasoline and oil changes
  • Tire wear and general maintenance
  • Vehicle depreciation and loss of value
  • Commercial auto insurance

If you drive 100 total miles during a shift, the IRS considers that a $76.00 business expense. You deduct this amount directly from your gross earnings to find your taxable profit.

3. The Self-Employment Tax Penalty

If your business shows a profit after the mileage deduction, you are subject to the 15.3% Self-Employment Tax on 92.35% of those net earnings. This tax is completely separate from your standard federal and state income tax obligations.

Because of this heavy tax burden combined with invisible vehicle depreciation, many drivers who believe they are earning $20/hour discover their True Net Wage is closer to minimum wage once the math is fully accounted for.
