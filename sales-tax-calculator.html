What is sales tax?

Sales tax is a consumption tax charged on qualifying retail sales. In the United States, it is generally imposed by states and local governments rather than by a single nationwide sales tax. The rate you see at checkout can depend on the state, county, city, district, the type of product or service, and the rules that apply to the transaction.

That is why a $100 item can cost more than $100 at checkout even when the shelf price is exactly $100. This sales tax calculator lets you enter the rate that applies to your purchase and see the math instantly.

How to calculate sales tax

Convert the percentage to a decimal, multiply it by the pre-tax price, and add the result to the original price.

Sales tax = pre-tax price × (tax rate ÷ 100)
Total price = pre-tax price + sales tax

Example: at 7.25% sales tax, a $100 purchase creates $7.25 in tax, so the final price is $107.25.

How to add sales tax to a price

Use Add sales tax when you know the original price and the sales tax rate. The calculator returns the tax amount and the final total. This is useful for checking a purchase, estimating a final checkout price, comparing budgets, or answering questions such as how much a $12, $21.99, $54.99, or $100 item will cost after tax.

How to back out sales tax from a total

If you already have a tax-included total, you cannot simply subtract the tax rate from the total. The rate was originally applied to the smaller pre-tax price, so the correct calculation divides the total by one plus the rate.

Pre-tax price = tax-included total ÷ (1 + tax rate ÷ 100)
Tax portion = tax-included total − pre-tax price

For example, if a receipt total is $107.25 and the sales tax rate is 7.25%, the pre-tax price is $100 and the tax portion is $7.25. This is sometimes called reversing sales tax, removing sales tax, or finding the price before tax.

How to find the sales tax rate

Sometimes you know the price before tax and the final price but do not know the tax rate. Choose Find tax rate and enter both amounts. The calculator subtracts the two prices to find the tax amount, then expresses that tax as a percentage of the pre-tax price.

Tax rate = (after-tax price − pre-tax price) ÷ pre-tax price × 100

For example, a $100 item that becomes $107.25 has a $7.25 tax amount. $7.25 ÷ $100 = 0.0725, or 7.25%.

How much is $100 plus sales tax?

At a 7.25% rate, $100 plus sales tax is $107.25. At a different rate, the answer changes. Enter the exact rate that applies to your purchase instead of assuming the statewide rate is the final checkout rate.

The same calculation works for small and large purchases, including $1.50, $1.75, $5, $12, $13.50, $15, $21.99, $22, $49.99, $54.99, $200, $500, and $1,000.

Why your local sales tax rate may be different

The statewide rate is often only one part of the amount charged at checkout. Local governments may add taxes, and special districts can add another layer. Tax Foundation's July 2026 data shows meaningful differences between statewide rates and population-weighted combined rates in many states.

For example, California has a 7.25% statewide rate while its average combined rate is higher. Colorado has a 2.90% statewide rate but a much higher average combined rate because of local taxes. Louisiana has a 5.00% statewide rate and a 10.13% average combined rate in the July 2026 snapshot. These are broad reference figures, not a quote for a particular address.

Sales tax vs. VAT

Sales tax and value-added tax (VAT) both affect the final cost of goods and services, but they are collected differently. U.S. retail sales tax is generally imposed on the final consumer transaction. VAT is collected through multiple stages of production and distribution and is commonly included in the displayed consumer price.

That difference matters when comparing a U.S. price with a price in a country where VAT is already included. A $100 shelf price can mean different things depending on the tax system.

Why the tax on a receipt can surprise you

A tax rate can be correct and still look different from what you expected because the taxable amount may not be the same as the sticker price. Discounts, shipping, product category rules, exemptions, local taxes, and special taxes can affect the final calculation.

This calculator assumes the amount you enter is the taxable base and that the rate you enter applies to that amount. For unusual transactions or official filings, confirm the rules with the relevant tax authority.

Taxable, exempt, and reduced-rate purchases

Not every product is treated the same way in every jurisdiction. Some states exempt or reduce tax on certain groceries, clothing, prescription medicines, or other categories. Digital goods and services can also have specific rules.

That means a general sales tax calculator should not be treated as a legal determination that a particular item is taxable. Use the exact rate and taxability rules that apply to your purchase.

How to use the sales tax calculator

  1. Add sales tax: enter the pre-tax price and rate to calculate tax and the final price.
  2. Remove sales tax: enter the tax-included total and rate to find the original price and tax portion.
  3. Find tax rate: enter the pre-tax and after-tax prices to calculate the implied sales tax rate.
  4. Check the rate: use the state table as a reference, then confirm the rate for the exact location and item when accuracy matters.

Sources and methodology

The calculator uses the standard formulas shown on this page. It does not connect to a tax authority and does not determine whether a specific transaction is taxable. Tax Foundation notes that its local figures are population-weighted averages and that city, county, and municipal rates vary.

Sales tax rules can change during the year. Verify the current rate and product-specific rules with the relevant state or local tax authority before using the result for an official filing, invoice, or regulated transaction.
