How much should you tip?

Tipping norms vary by country, service type, and quality of service. Here are the standard guidelines:

  • Restaurants (US): 15% for adequate service, 18% for good service, 20–25% for excellent service
  • Takeout (US): 10% is appreciated but optional
  • Bars (US): $1–2 per drink, or 15–20% of the tab
  • Taxis and rideshare (US): 15–20%
  • Hotel housekeeping: $2–5 per night
  • UK and Europe: Tipping is less expected — 10% is generous, some restaurants include a service charge already
  • Japan: Tipping is generally not customary and can even be considered rude

How to calculate a tip quickly in your head

  • 10% tip: Move the decimal point one place left. $85 becomes $8.50
  • 15% tip: Find 10%, then add half of that. $8.50 + $4.25 = $12.75
  • 20% tip: Find 10% and double it. $8.50 x 2 = $17.00

Should you tip on the pre-tax or post-tax amount?

Etiquette experts say tip on the pre-tax amount — there is no reason to tip the government's share. In practice most people tip on the post-tax total since that is the number shown on the bill. Either is acceptable — the difference on a typical meal is usually under a dollar.

How does unequal bill splitting work?
