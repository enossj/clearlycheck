How to Calculate a Tip & Split Checks Accurately

Figuring out the right tip at a restaurant, coffee shop, or salon does not have to involve guesswork. The formula behind every gratuity is straightforward, and once you understand it you can apply it to any situation — whether you are dining solo or splitting a group check five ways.

The Tip Formula

Every tip calculation starts with the same core equation:

Tip Amount = Bill Amount × Tip Percentage ÷ 100
Grand Total = Bill Amount + Tip Amount
Per Person = Grand Total ÷ Number of People

That is it. The only decision you need to make is what percentage to leave, and this calculator handles the rest instantly.

Step-by-Step Example

Imagine you and a friend finish a dinner that costs $65.00 before tip. You want to leave an 18% tip and split everything evenly between 2 people. Here is how the math works:

  • Step 1 — Calculate the tip: $65.00 × 18 ÷ 100 = $11.70
  • Step 2 — Find the grand total: $65.00 + $11.70 = $76.70
  • Step 3 — Split between 2 people: $76.70 ÷ 2 = $38.35 each

Each person pays $38.35, which covers their half of the food plus their fair share of the tip. Enter $65, select 18%, and set 2 people in the calculator above to verify this instantly.

Quick Mental Math Shortcuts

You do not always need a calculator. These tricks let you estimate a tip in seconds at the table:

  • 10% tip: Move the decimal one place left. A $72.00 bill becomes $7.20.
  • 15% tip: Find 10% first, then add half of that. $7.20 + $3.60 = $10.80.
  • 20% tip: Find 10% and double it. $7.20 × 2 = $14.40.
  • 25% tip: Find 10%, double it, then add another half. $7.20 + $7.20 + $3.60 = $18.00.

Standard Tipping Etiquette by Industry

Tipping expectations differ significantly depending on the type of service. Below is a comprehensive breakdown of current US norms across the most common categories.

Sit-Down Restaurants
15% is the baseline for adequate service, 18% for good service, and 20–25% for excellent or complex service (large parties, special accommodations). Many restaurants automatically add an 18–20% gratuity for parties of six or more — check your bill before adding extra.

Food Delivery Drivers
15–20% of the order total is standard. For small orders under $20, tip a minimum of $3–5. Tip more during bad weather, late-night hours, or when the driver navigates stairs, apartment buildings, or other difficult drop-off situations. Remember that delivery fees charged by apps typically do not go to the driver.

Coffee Shops & Counter Service
Tipping at counter-service establishments is generally optional but increasingly expected in the US. A tip of $1–2 per drink or 10–15% is appropriate for custom espresso drinks, smoothies, or large orders. For a simple drip coffee, tipping is entirely at your discretion.

Hair Salons & Spas
15–20% of the service cost is customary for stylists, barbers, colorists, and massage therapists. If multiple specialists work on your visit (e.g., a colorist and a separate stylist), tip each person individually based on their share of the total service price. Shampoo assistants who wash and blow-dry your hair typically receive $3–5 separately.

Hotel Housekeeping
The American Hotel & Lodging Association recommends $1–5 per night depending on room type and service level. Leave the tip daily rather than at checkout — different housekeepers may clean your room on different days. Place cash in a clearly marked envelope or with a note so the staff knows it is intended for them.

Should You Tip on the Pre-Tax or Post-Tax Amount?

Etiquette experts recommend tipping on the pre-tax subtotal since the sales tax goes to the government, not the server. In practice, however, most diners tip on the post-tax total because that is the final number printed on the bill. Either approach is perfectly acceptable — the difference on a typical restaurant meal is usually less than a dollar. This calculator uses whatever bill amount you enter, so you can input either the pre-tax or post-tax figure according to your preference.

How Does Unequal Bill Splitting Work?

Switch to Custom amounts mode above and enter what each person ordered before tip. The calculator distributes the tip proportionally — if Person A ordered 60% of the food, they pay 60% of the tip. This ensures everyone pays exactly their fair share without anyone subsidizing someone else's lobster dinner.
