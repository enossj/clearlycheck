How is salary converted to hourly?

The conversion is straightforward: divide your annual salary by the total number of hours worked in a year. For a standard full-time worker:

Hourly rate = Annual salary / (Hours per week x Weeks per year)

Example: $65,000 / (40 x 52) = $65,000 / 2,080 = $31.25/hour

This gives you your gross hourly rate — before taxes and deductions. Your actual take-home will be lower depending on your tax bracket, country, and any pre-tax deductions like pension contributions.

What counts as full-time hours?

In most countries, a standard full-time job is 40 hours per week across 52 weeks, totalling 2,080 hours per year. However, many salaried employees work more than this without extra pay. If you regularly work 45-50 hours, your effective hourly rate is lower than the calculator shows at 40 hours.

  • 40 hrs x 52 weeks = 2,080 hours/year (standard full-time)
  • 37.5 hrs x 52 weeks = 1,950 hours/year (common in UK)
  • 40 hrs x 50 weeks = 2,000 hours/year (after 2 weeks leave)

What is the difference between gross and net pay?

Gross pay is what your employer pays you before any deductions. Net pay (take-home) is what lands in your bank account after income tax, social security, and any other deductions are taken out. The take-home estimate uses simplified effective tax rates and is a rough guide only.

Freelancers: charge more than you think

If you are a freelancer comparing yourself to an employed worker, remember that a salaried employee receives benefits their employer pays — pension contributions, health insurance, paid leave, and sick days. To match a $50,000 salaried role as a freelancer, you typically need to charge 30-50% more to account for these costs, taxes, and unpaid downtime between projects.

Is this wage competitive?

Context is everything. A wage that is comfortable in one city can be below living wage in another. The best way to benchmark your pay is to compare against industry salary surveys for your role, experience level, and location — sites like Glassdoor, Levels.fyi, and LinkedIn Salary are useful starting points.
