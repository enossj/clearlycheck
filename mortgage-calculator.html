How Much Is My Mortgage Payment?

A mortgage calculator estimates the cost of financing a home by combining your loan amount, interest rate, loan term, and the costs that may be part of your monthly housing payment. This calculator also lets you include property taxes, homeowners insurance, PMI, HOA fees, other costs, and extra principal payments.

What Is Included in a Mortgage Payment?

Your principal-and-interest payment is the amount used to repay the mortgage itself. Your total monthly housing payment can be higher when taxes, insurance, mortgage insurance, HOA dues, or other recurring costs are included.

Component What it means
Principal The portion of the payment that reduces the amount you borrowed.
Interest The borrowing cost charged on the remaining mortgage balance.
Property tax Real estate taxes charged by your local government. They may be collected through an escrow account.
Homeowners insurance Insurance that helps protect the property against covered losses and may be included in an escrow payment.
PMI Private mortgage insurance that may apply to some conventional loans when the down payment is below 20%.
HOA Homeowners association dues, when applicable. HOA fees are generally separate from principal and interest.

What Is PITI?

PITI stands for Principal, Interest, Taxes, and Insurance. A PITI mortgage calculator helps estimate a broader monthly housing payment rather than showing only the mortgage principal-and-interest amount. PMI, HOA fees, and other costs can be added separately.

How to Calculate a Mortgage Payment

For a fixed-rate mortgage, the principal-and-interest payment is calculated from the loan amount, monthly interest rate, and number of monthly payments. The standard amortization formula spreads repayment across the selected loan term. This calculator then adds the monthly portions of property taxes, homeowners insurance, PMI, HOA fees, and other costs that you enter.

Your loan amount is generally the home price minus the down payment. A larger down payment reduces the amount borrowed and can also affect whether mortgage insurance is required.

Mortgage Calculator With Taxes and Insurance

Looking only at principal and interest can understate the amount you actually spend each month on housing. Enter your annual property tax and homeowners insurance to estimate a more complete monthly payment. Because tax rates and insurance premiums vary by location and property, use your actual estimates when available.

Mortgage Calculator With PMI

PMI can increase the monthly cost of a conventional mortgage when it applies. The amount and cancellation rules depend on the loan, lender, borrower, and applicable requirements. Enter the estimated annual PMI cost in the calculator to include it in your monthly payment.

How Mortgage Amortization Works

A mortgage amortization schedule shows how each scheduled payment changes your loan balance. Early in a typical fixed-rate mortgage, interest represents a larger share of the payment because interest is calculated from a larger outstanding balance. As principal is repaid, the balance falls and the interest portion generally decreases.

Use the annual view for a quick long-term summary or the monthly view to inspect the first 24 payments. The schedule also reflects the extra payments entered in the calculator.

How Much Interest Will I Pay on My Mortgage?

Total interest depends mainly on the amount borrowed, interest rate, and how long the balance remains outstanding. A longer loan term usually produces a lower required monthly principal-and-interest payment but can result in more interest over the life of the loan. The calculator shows estimated total interest based on the inputs you provide.

How Extra Mortgage Payments Can Save Interest

Extra principal payments reduce the outstanding balance. A lower balance generally means less interest is charged in subsequent periods, which can shorten the payoff timeline and reduce total interest. This calculator lets you test extra monthly payments, extra yearly payments, and a one-time lump sum.

Compare the standard schedule with the extra-payment scenario to see the estimated time and interest savings. Check your mortgage terms before making additional payments because some loans can have specific rules or fees.

15-Year vs. 30-Year Mortgage

A 15-year mortgage generally requires a higher monthly principal-and-interest payment than a 30-year mortgage for the same loan amount and rate, but it pays the balance down over a shorter period. A 30-year mortgage generally spreads repayment over more months. Your actual choice depends on your rate, budget, goals, and loan terms.
