How to Calculate and Track Your Net Worth

A high salary does not automatically equal true wealth. To understand your actual financial health, you need to track your net worth. Here is exactly what counts as an asset, what counts as a liability, and how to measure your progress.

Society often equates financial success with a large paycheck. We see someone driving a luxury vehicle or living in a massive home and immediately assume they are wealthy. However, income only measures how much money flows into your bank account. It tells you nothing about how much money actually stays there.

If you earn $200,000 a year but spend $195,000 on lifestyle expenses and debt payments, you are highly dependent on your next paycheck. True wealth is measured by your net worth. It is the ultimate financial scorecard.

The Simple Formula

Your net worth is essentially a snapshot of your financial life at a specific moment in time. The formula is incredibly straightforward.

Total Assets - Total Liabilities = Net Worth

If you sold everything you owned today and used that cash to pay off every single debt you owe, the money left over is your net worth. If the number is positive, you own more than you owe. If the number is negative, you owe more than you own.

What Counts as an Asset?

An asset is anything you own that holds significant monetary value. When adding up your assets, you should focus on items that could reasonably be converted into cash. We typically divide these into three categories.

  • Cash and Equivalents: Checking accounts, savings accounts, emergency funds, and physical cash.
  • Investments: Retirement accounts like a 401(k) or IRA, standard brokerage accounts, stocks, bonds, and mutual funds.
  • Real Property: The current market value of your primary residence, investment properties, and land.

A very common question is whether you should include your cars, furniture, or jewelry. You can technically include them, but you must be careful. Cars are depreciating assets. Their value drops constantly. If you choose to list your vehicle as an asset, you must use its current trade-in value, not the price you originally paid for it.

What Counts as a Liability?

A liability is any money you owe to another person or institution. You must be completely honest when totaling your liabilities, or your net worth calculation will be useless.

  • Secured Debt: Your home mortgage balance and your auto loan balances. Note that you list the total remaining loan balance, not just your monthly payment.
  • Unsecured Debt: Credit card balances, personal loans, and payday loans.
  • Educational Debt: The total outstanding balance of all student loans.
  • Tax Liabilities: Any back taxes owed to the government.
The House Equity Example:
If your house is worth $400,000 and you owe $300,000 on the mortgage, your house is an asset ($400k) and your mortgage is a liability ($300k). The net positive impact on your total wealth is the $100,000 in equity.

Liquid vs. Total Net Worth

As your finances grow, tracking a single number is no longer enough. You need to understand the difference between your total wealth and your liquid wealth.

Total Net Worth includes absolutely everything. It includes the equity in your house and the balance of your retirement accounts. This is a great metric for measuring your overall financial trajectory.

Liquid Net Worth excludes assets that are difficult to sell quickly. You cannot buy groceries with home equity, and withdrawing money from a 401(k) early triggers severe tax penalties. Liquid net worth only counts cash, high-yield savings, and standard taxable investment accounts. This number tells you exactly how much accessible capital you have in case of a major life emergency.

Average Net Worth by Age

People naturally want to know how they compare to their peers. It is completely normal to have a negative net worth in your twenties, especially if you took out student loans to fund your education. Wealth building takes decades.

When looking at benchmarks, you should always look at the median rather than the average. Extremely wealthy individuals heavily skew the average upward. The median represents the exact middle point of the population.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does my house count toward my net worth?
Yes. You count the current market value of your house as an asset, and you count the remaining balance of your mortgage as a liability. The difference between the two is your home equity, which directly increases your total net worth.
Should I include my car as an asset?
You can include it, but you should use the current Kelley Blue Book trade-in value. Cars are depreciating assets, meaning they lose value every month. If your auto loan balance is higher than the car's current value, the vehicle is negatively impacting your net worth.
Why is my net worth negative?
A negative net worth simply means your total debts are larger than your total assets. This is incredibly common for recent college graduates who have large student loans but have not had time to build savings or buy property yet.
Should I include my 401(k) or IRA?
Yes. Your retirement accounts are major assets and should absolutely be included in your total net worth. However, they should generally be excluded when you are calculating your liquid net worth, due to the penalties associated with early withdrawal.
Do I subtract future taxes from my net worth?
For a basic personal net worth calculation, most people do not calculate future tax liabilities on their retirement accounts or unsold property. However, highly detailed financial planners will sometimes calculate a post-tax net worth to show a more conservative estimate of available wealth.
How often should I calculate my net worth?
Tracking it once a quarter or twice a year is plenty for most people. Tracking it daily or weekly can cause unnecessary stress due to normal stock market fluctuations. The goal is to monitor the long-term trend, not daily changes.
Do my personal belongings like electronics and furniture count?
Most financial experts advise ignoring smaller personal items. Unless you own fine art, rare collectibles, or expensive jewelry that holds objective market value, trying to calculate the resale value of a used couch or television just clutters your math.
What is a good net worth by age 30?
A common financial rule of thumb suggests aiming to have a net worth equal to half of your annual salary by age 30. If you earn $60,000 a year, hitting a $30,000 net worth shows you are successfully paying down debt and building early investments.
Is my salary part of my net worth?
No. Your salary is income, not an asset. Income only increases your net worth if you successfully save or invest that money rather than spending it.
Does getting a loan increase my net worth?
Taking out a personal cash loan increases your assets (cash in hand) but equally increases your liabilities (the money owed). The immediate net effect is zero. If you spend that loan money on a vacation, your asset disappears, leaving only the liability, which decreases your net worth.
