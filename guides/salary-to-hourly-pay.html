Salary to Hourly: How to Calculate Your True Pay

Comparing a salary offer to an hourly job can be incredibly confusing. We break down the exact math needed to figure out exactly how much you make per hour so you can budget accurately and negotiate better offers.

When you sit down to create a monthly budget or consider a new job offer, you need to know exactly what your time is worth. If you are offered an annual salary of $60,000, it is very difficult to compare that directly to an hourly job offering $28 an hour without doing some specific math.

The standard way to figure out your hourly rate is by using what human resource professionals call the 2,080-hour rule. This number represents a standard, full-time working year in the United States.

How We Get 2,080 Hours:
• 40 hours worked per week
• Multiplied by 52 weeks in a calendar year
• Equals exactly 2,080 standard working hours.

To find your hourly wage, you simply take your gross annual salary before taxes and divide it by 2,080.

The Calculation: Hourly Wage = Annual Salary ÷ 2,080 Example: $50,000 ÷ 2,080 = $24.04 per hour

2,080 Hours vs. 2,000 Hours

While 2,080 is the standard corporate calculation, some workers prefer to use a 2,000-hour base instead. This lower number provides a slightly different perspective on your earning power.

  • The 2,080-Hour Model: This assumes you are paid for every single hour of a 40-hour week. It includes paid time off and paid holidays.
  • The 2,000-Hour Model: This model subtracts unpaid time. If you take two full weeks of unpaid vacation per year, you only work 50 weeks. At 40 hours a week, that equals 2,000 hours. Dividing your salary by 2,000 results in a slightly higher calculated hourly rate.

The Hidden Value of a Salary

When comparing an hourly rate to a salary offer, the base math does not tell the whole story. Salaried positions frequently include a Total Compensation Package that drastically increases your actual earnings.

  • Health Insurance Subsidies: Employers often pay a large portion of your monthly health, dental, and vision insurance premiums. This can save you thousands of dollars a year compared to buying a private plan as an independent hourly contractor.
  • 401(k) Matching: If your company matches 3% of your retirement contributions, that is essentially free money added on top of your base salary.
  • Paid Time Off (PTO): Salaried employees are typically granted paid sick days, vacation days, and federal holidays. You continue to earn your standard rate even while you are sitting on a beach.
  • Performance Bonuses: Many corporate salary structures include end-of-year bonuses or profit-sharing options that are rarely available to hourly wage earners.

Gross Pay vs. Net Pay

The numbers listed in the chart above represent your Gross Pay. This is the total amount of money your employer pays you before the government steps in.

Your Net Pay is the actual amount of money that gets deposited into your bank account on payday. Taxes will take a massive bite out of your gross hourly rate. Depending on your state, you must account for Federal Income Tax, State Income Tax, Medicare, and Social Security (FICA) deductions. A $50,000 salary might look like $4,167 a month on paper, but after taxes and insurance premiums, your take-home pay might only be $3,200.

Salary vs. Hourly Differences

Beyond the math, there are structural lifestyle differences between being an hourly worker and a salaried employee.

The Salaried Advantage: Being on a salary provides tremendous financial stability. Your paycheck is the exact same size every single pay period. If the office closes early on a Friday, your income does not drop. This predictability makes paying rent and managing subscriptions much easier.

The Hourly Advantage: Hourly workers are strictly compensated for the exact amount of time they give the company. When you clock out, you are entirely disconnected from work. If management asks you to stay late, your paycheck physically grows. Salaried workers often find themselves working 45 to 50 hours a week without seeing a single extra dime.

Understanding Overtime

By federal law in the United States, eligible hourly workers must receive overtime pay if they work more than 40 hours in a single workweek. Overtime is calculated at a rate of time and a half.

If your standard hourly rate is $20.00, your overtime rate becomes $30.00 for every extra hour you work. This means an hourly job with frequent overtime opportunities can often outpace a seemingly higher salaried job over the course of a year. Most salaried employees are considered "exempt" and do not qualify for overtime pay.

Hourly to Salary (Reverse Math)

If you are currently working an hourly job and want to know what your equivalent annual salary is, you just reverse the standard calculation. You multiply your hourly rate by the number of hours you work in a year.

The Reverse Calculation: Annual Salary = Hourly Wage × 2,080 Example: $25.00 per hour × 2,080 = $52,000 a year

Frequently Asked Questions

How much is $50k a year hourly?
If you work a standard 40-hour week for 52 weeks, a $50,000 salary equals exactly $24.04 per hour.
Does my salary calculation include paid time off?
Yes. Because you are paid your standard rate even when taking vacation or sick days, those hours are included in the 2,080 hour working year base calculation.
What is time and a half?
Time and a half is the standard overtime premium required by law for eligible hourly workers. You take your base hourly pay and multiply it by 1.5 to find your overtime rate.
How many working days are in a year?
There are generally 260 standard working days in a calendar year. This excludes weekends but includes federal holidays.
Is a $60,000 salary considered good?
It largely depends on your location. A $60k salary is slightly above the national average and can provide a comfortable life in the Midwest, but it will stretch your budget very thin in high-cost cities like San Francisco or New York.
Do salaried employees get overtime?
Usually no. Most salaried employees are considered exempt under the Fair Labor Standards Act, meaning they are not legally entitled to overtime pay regardless of how many hours they work.
How do I calculate my bi-weekly pay?
If you are paid bi-weekly, you will receive 26 paychecks in a year. Simply take your total annual salary and divide it by 26 to find your gross bi-weekly pay amount before taxes.
What is the formula to convert hourly to salary?
To reverse the math, take your hourly rate and multiply it by 2,080. If you make $25 an hour, multiplying it by 2,080 equals an annual salary of $52,000.
Is it better to be paid salary or hourly?
Neither is universally better. Salary offers stability and benefits, while hourly pay guarantees you are fairly compensated for every single hour you dedicate to the company. It depends on your lifestyle preferences.
How much is $100k a year hourly?
A six-figure salary of $100,000 translates to an hourly rate of exactly $48.08 based on a standard 40-hour work week.
