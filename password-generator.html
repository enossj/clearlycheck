How this password generator works

This tool creates passwords using your browser's cryptographically secure random number generator (window.crypto.getRandomValues), not a weaker pseudo-random function. That distinction matters because predictable randomness is exactly what attackers look for when trying to guess or brute-force passwords. Everything happens locally on your device — nothing is transmitted, logged, or stored anywhere, so the password you generate here is never seen by our servers.

Why password length matters more than complexity

Many people assume a short password packed with symbols is safer than a long one made of ordinary characters, but the math says otherwise. Each additional character multiplies the number of possible combinations an attacker has to try. A random 10-character password might take hours to brute-force, while a random 16-character password can take centuries — even using only letters and numbers. That's why modern guidance favors "long and random" over "short and complex."

Best practices for password security

  • Use a unique password for every account. If one site is breached, reused passwords let attackers into your other accounts through credential stuffing.
  • Aim for 16+ characters on anything important — email, banking, your password manager's master password.
  • Use a password manager so you don't have to remember dozens of random strings. You only need to remember one strong master password.
  • Turn on two-factor authentication (2FA) wherever it's offered. It protects you even if a password is ever exposed.
  • Avoid personal information like names, birthdays, or pet names — these are often guessable or discoverable from social media.
  • Change passwords immediately if a service you use reports a data breach.
