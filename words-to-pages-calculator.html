How word-to-page estimates work

A page count is really just a word count divided by however many words fit on one page — the tricky part is that "how many words fit on one page" changes depending on the font, size, and spacing you use. This calculator starts from a base rate of 500 words per page at 12-point Times New Roman single-spaced, then applies three adjustment factors: one for the font's average character width, one for font size, and one for line spacing. Multiply the base rate by all three factors and you get the words-per-page figure used to convert your word count into pages, or your page count back into words.

Why font, size, and spacing all matter

Line spacing has the biggest effect: double-spacing roughly halves how many lines fit on a page compared to single-spacing, and 1.5 spacing lands in between. Font size matters too — dropping from 12pt to 11pt lets about 10% more text fit in the same space, since each line of text takes up slightly less vertical room. Font choice is the subtlest factor: Times New Roman and Arial occupy roughly the same amount of space per word, Calibri is a touch narrower and fits about 5% more words per page, and Courier New is a fixed-width (monospace) font that reserves the same width for every character, so it fits noticeably fewer words per page — about 25% less than Times New Roman at the same size and spacing.

Real length still varies

These figures are typography averages, not a guarantee of how many pages your finished document will run to. Titles, headings, block quotes, bullet lists, tables, images, and the paragraph breaks between sections all use space without holding a proportional number of words, so a real document with lots of structure will usually run a little longer on paper than the raw word-count math predicts. A dense wall of continuous prose, on the other hand, will land close to the estimate. Use the numbers here as a solid planning figure — for a length requirement your instructor or editor will actually check, always confirm against the formatted document itself.
