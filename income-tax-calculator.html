How this 2026 federal income tax calculator works

Most people start by looking at their salary, but a tax bill is built from several steps. Your wages and other taxable income form gross income. Eligible adjustments can reduce that amount to adjusted gross income (AGI). You then generally take either the standard deduction or itemized deductions, and certain additional deductions may apply. The remaining taxable income is what the federal tax brackets are applied to.

The key idea: your salary, taxable income, tax liability and tax refund are four different numbers. Understanding the difference makes tax estimates much easier to follow.

What is taxable income?

Taxable income is the amount left after the deductions and adjustments included in the calculation. It is taxable income, not gross salary, that moves through the federal income-tax brackets. That is why two people with the same salary can have different federal tax bills.

2026 federal income tax brackets

The United States uses a progressive tax system. Each portion of taxable income is taxed at its bracket rate. Moving into a higher bracket does not mean your entire income suddenly gets taxed at that higher rate.

Standard deduction for 2026

For 2026, the standard deduction is $16,100 for single taxpayers and married taxpayers filing separately, $32,200 for married couples filing jointly and qualifying surviving spouses, and $24,150 for heads of household. Additional amounts may apply for taxpayers who are 65 or older or legally blind.

What tax bracket am I in?

Your marginal tax bracket is the highest rate reached by your taxable income. It is easy to confuse this with your effective tax rate, but they answer different questions.

Marginal rate tells you the rate on the next dollars of taxable income that fall into your current bracket. Effective rate compares the tax produced by the calculator with your gross income. Because lower portions of income are taxed at lower rates, the effective rate is generally lower.

How your taxable income is calculated

Gross income
− eligible adjustments
= Adjusted gross income (AGI)
− standard or itemized deduction
− eligible additional deductions
= Taxable income

That taxable income then moves through the federal tax brackets. Credits come after the tax calculation and can reduce the amount of tax you owe. Refundable credits can also increase a refund when the credit is larger than the remaining tax liability.

Tax refund vs. amount owed

A refund does not mean you paid no tax. It generally means more money was already paid through withholding or estimated payments than the amount ultimately due after credits.

Estimated refund = withholding + estimated payments + refundable credits − final estimated tax

If the result is negative, the calculator shows an estimated amount owed instead. Your actual refund can differ because a real return includes many forms, elections, credits and special rules that this simplified calculator does not model.

Child Tax Credit and Credit for Other Dependents

The maximum Child Tax Credit is $2,200 per qualifying child, with a refundable Additional Child Tax Credit component that can be worth up to $1,700 per qualifying child under the applicable rules. The Credit for Other Dependents can be up to $500. Income phaseouts and qualification rules apply.

New 2026 deductions you may have heard about

Federal tax rules for 2026 include several deductions introduced or expanded under recent legislation. They are not automatic benefits for everyone. Each has its own definition, income limits and eligibility tests. The IRS says taxpayers may be able to claim deductions for qualified tips, qualified overtime, qualifying passenger vehicle loan interest and an enhanced senior deduction.

Qualified tipsUp to $25,000 may be deductible for qualifying workers, subject to income limits and reporting rules.
Qualified overtimeUp to $12,500, or $25,000 for joint filers, may be deductible when the overtime meets the federal definition.
Vehicle loan interestUp to $10,000 of qualifying passenger vehicle loan interest may be deductible, subject to requirements and income limits.
Enhanced senior deductionEligible taxpayers age 65 or older may qualify for an additional $6,000 deduction, subject to phaseouts and other requirements.

2026 charitable deduction for taxpayers who do not itemize

Starting in 2026, eligible non-itemizers can claim a deduction for qualifying cash contributions, subject to an annual limit and specific rules. The calculator treats this as an optional planning estimate rather than a full Schedule 1-A determination.

Income tax vs. tax withholding

Your income tax is the amount calculated from your taxable income and the applicable tax rules. Withholding is money already sent to the government during the year. This is why your tax return can end with either a refund or a balance due even when you had tax withheld from every paycheck.

W-2 income vs. self-employment income

This tool focuses on federal income tax. A self-employed person can also owe self-employment tax and may have business deductions that require a more detailed calculation. For that reason, the result should not be treated as a full 1099 tax estimate.
