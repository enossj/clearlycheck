How does the scam checker work?

This tool uses AI to analyse the text, URL, phone number, or email you paste and look for known scam patterns and risk indicators. It checks things like domain age and structure for URLs, number format and origin for phone numbers, sender patterns for emails, and linguistic patterns commonly used in fraud attempts for messages.

The risk score (0–100) reflects the likelihood that what you've shared has characteristics associated with scam activity. A high score does not guarantee it is a scam — and a low score does not guarantee it is safe. Use this as one tool among several, and always trust your instincts.

Common scam warning signs

  • Urgency and pressure — "Act now or lose your account," "Only 24 hours left," "Immediate action required."
  • Too good to be true — Unexpected prize wins, lottery results, inheritance notices, or job offers with unusually high pay.
  • Unusual payment requests — Gift cards, wire transfers, cryptocurrency, or payment apps like Zelle for goods or services.
  • Suspicious URLs — Slight misspellings of real sites (paypa1.com, arnazon.com, g00gle.com), random character strings, or unfamiliar domains.
  • Requests for personal info — Social security numbers, bank account details, passwords, or OTP codes.
  • Unsolicited contact — Being contacted out of nowhere about a package, fine, tax debt, or prize you were not expecting.

What to do if you think you have been scammed

  • Stop contact immediately. Do not reply, click links, or send more money.
  • Contact your bank if you shared any financial information or made a payment.
  • Change passwords for any accounts you may have shared credentials for.
  • Report it: In the US, report to the FTC at reportfraud.ftc.gov. In the UK, report to Action Fraud at actionfraud.police.uk.
  • Warn others — share the scammer's details with family and friends so they are not targeted next.
