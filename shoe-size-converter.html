How shoe sizing works

Every shoe size system is really a way of describing foot length — but each region measures and labels it differently, which is why the numbers rarely match. US and UK sizes use a unit called the barleycorn (one third of an inch) but start counting from different points, so they're about 1.5 sizes apart. EU sizes use Paris points (two thirds of a centimetre) and are close to unisex. Centimetres describe the foot length directly and are the most reliable anchor of all.

The four systems at a glance

  • US — separate men's and women's scales; a women's size is about 1.5 higher than the men's size for the same foot.
  • UK — the origin of the US system; a UK size is roughly 0.5 below the equivalent US men's size and about 2 below the US women's size.
  • EU — based on Paris points and largely unisex; the same EU number can be labelled for men or women.
  • CM (Mondopoint) — the actual length of your foot in centimetres, used on athletic and hiking footwear and the safest cross-brand reference.

How to measure your foot

  1. Put a sheet of paper flat against a wall and stand on it with your heel touching the wall.
  2. Mark the paper at the tip of your longest toe (which isn't always the big toe).
  3. Measure from the wall edge of the paper to that mark in centimetres.
  4. Do both feet in the afternoon, when feet are at their largest, and use the bigger measurement.

Add about 0.5-1 cm of wiggle room for a comfortable fit, then match that length to the chart above.

Why sizes still vary between brands

Even with a perfect conversion, two shoes labelled the same size can fit differently because each brand builds around its own last — the foot-shaped mould that sets length, width, and toe shape. Running brands often run small, some fashion brands run large, and width isn't captured by the number at all. Knowing your foot length in centimetres and checking a brand's own size guide beats trusting the label alone.

Sources & method

This converter maps between US, UK, EU, and centimetre sizes using standard published conversion charts based on foot length; because the systems are defined differently and brands vary, values are matched to the nearest standard size and are approximate. Foot length in centimetres (Mondopoint) is the most reliable measure. For children's sizing, medical foot conditions, or orthotics, a professional shoe fitter or podiatrist is the right resource.
