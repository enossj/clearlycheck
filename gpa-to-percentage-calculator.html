What GPA and percentage grades actually measure

A percentage grade measures exactly how much of the possible credit you earned on a specific piece of work — 85 out of 100 points is 85%, full stop. A GPA (grade point average) is a different kind of number: it converts a letter grade into a point value on a 4.0 scale and then averages those points across every class you've taken, usually weighted by credit hours. The two describe related but different things — one is a raw score, the other is a standardized summary used to compare performance across different classes, teachers, and even schools.

Why converting GPA to a percentage gives a range, not a single number

Going from a percentage to a GPA is exact — any specific percentage falls into exactly one grade band, so 85% is always a B worth 3.0. Going the other way is not exact, because a single GPA point represents an entire band of percentages. A 4.0 GPA could reflect a 93% or a 100% — both are an A. So this calculator reports the representative letter grade along with the full percentage range that maps to it, rather than pretending there's one true percentage hiding behind every GPA.

Institutions map grades to points differently

The GPA-to-percentage scale below reflects the most common US convention: a 10-point scale with plus and minus grades, where an A- is worth 3.7 and covers 90–92%. But this is a convention, not a universal standard. Some schools use a simpler scale with no plus or minus grades at all, where anything 90% and up is a flat 4.0. Others shift individual cutoffs by a point or two, or use a 100-point scale that maps differently outside the US. Always check your own school's official grading policy — the syllabus or registrar's office — before treating this conversion as exact for your transcript.
