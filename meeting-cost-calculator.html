How the meeting cost is calculated

The math is deliberately simple: cost = attendees × average hourly pay × hours. If you enter an annual salary instead, it's converted to an hourly rate by dividing by 2,080 — the standard 40 hours × 52 weeks of full-time work. A 6-person, 1-hour meeting at $50 an hour costs 6 × $50 × 1 = $300. That's the salary cost of the time in the room, and it's the number most people never stop to add up.

Salary cost vs. the real cost

This tool shows base salary cost because it's the clearest and most defensible figure. The true cost to an organization is higher. Benefits, payroll taxes, software, and office space typically add 25–50% on top of salary — the "fully-loaded" cost. And that still ignores the hidden cost: the focus lost switching into and out of a meeting, and the work that didn't happen instead. To approximate the fully-loaded cost, raise the average rate you enter by a quarter to a half.

Recurring meetings are where the money is

A single meeting rarely looks expensive. The same meeting every week tells a different story. That $300 weekly sync is $15,600 a year — for one meeting on one team. Use the frequency selector to annualize it, and the numbers make the case for pruning standing meetings better than any argument. Ask of every recurring meeting: does it still need to exist, does it need everyone, and does it need the full hour?

Using the number well

The point isn't to ban meetings — good meetings are worth far more than they cost. The point is to make the invisible visible so the trade-off is deliberate. A $600 decision-making workshop that prevents a costly mistake is a bargain. A $600 weekly status update that could be a two-line message is not. Put the cost on the calendar invite and watch how quickly agendas tighten and guest lists shrink.

Cheaper meetings without losing value

  • Trim the guest list. Cost scales directly with headcount — invite deciders and doers, make everyone else optional.
  • Shorten the default. Many 60-minute meetings finish in 30 when the clock is shorter. Try 25 and 50 as defaults.
  • Kill or convert recurring meetings. Replace status updates with an async written summary; keep live time for discussion and decisions.
  • Always have an agenda and an owner. A clear goal and a decision-maker cut the time a meeting needs.

Sources & method

Cost is calculated as attendees × average hourly pay × meeting length in hours. Annualized figures multiply the single-meeting cost by the chosen frequency. Figures reflect base pay only; fully-loaded employer cost is typically 1.25–1.5× higher. All values stay in your browser.
