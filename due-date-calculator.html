How Is a Pregnancy Due Date Calculated?

A pregnancy due date calculator gives an estimated date of delivery (EDD), not a guaranteed birth date. The standard LMP method starts with the first day of your last menstrual period and estimates 40 weeks, or 280 days, of pregnancy. If your average menstrual cycle is shorter or longer than 28 days, the estimate can be adjusted.

This calculator also supports conception date, IVF embryo transfer, ultrasound dating, and a known due date. The method you choose changes how the pregnancy timeline is anchored, while the result remains an estimate for planning and education.

Method How It Is Estimated Useful When
Last Period (LMP) LMP + 280 days, adjusted for cycle length You know the first day of your last period.

How to Calculate Your Due Date From Your Last Period

The last menstrual period (LMP) method is one of the most common ways to estimate a due date. A standard 28-day cycle uses 280 days from the first day of the LMP. If your cycle is consistently longer or shorter, adjusting the calculation for cycle length can make the estimate more representative of your usual cycle.

For example, if your average cycle is 32 days, the calculator adds four additional days to the standard 280-day estimate. This is still only an estimate because ovulation does not occur on exactly the same day in every person or every cycle.

How Many Weeks Pregnant Am I?

Pregnancy is usually dated from the first day of the last menstrual period, rather than from conception. That means the clinical pregnancy clock starts before conception usually occurs. Enter your LMP, conception date, IVF transfer, or ultrasound information above and the calculator estimates your current gestational age in weeks and days.

The result also identifies your estimated trimester and shows important timeline dates, including the estimated conception date, trimester transitions, 39-week point, and estimated due date.

Pregnancy Due Date From Conception

If you know the conception date, the estimated due date is approximately 266 days after conception. Because conception is often difficult to pinpoint precisely, an ovulation or conception-based estimate can still have uncertainty.

IVF Due Date Calculator

For an IVF pregnancy, the embryo transfer date and embryo age can be used to establish pregnancy dating. This calculator supports both Day 3 embryo transfers and Day 5 blastocyst transfers. IVF dating is different from simply entering the transfer date as though it were conception.

Ultrasound Dating vs. LMP

An early pregnancy ultrasound can provide a more reliable estimate of gestational age when the LMP is uncertain or when the dates do not align. Your healthcare professional may revise the estimated due date based on ultrasound measurements and the timing of the scan. The NHS notes that a dating scan can give a more accurate estimate of how many weeks pregnant you are. NHS ultrasound guidance.

For that reason, treat an online calculator as a planning tool rather than a replacement for clinical dating. If your clinician has established a due date after an ultrasound or fertility treatment, use that clinical estimate when making healthcare decisions.

How Accurate Is a Pregnancy Due Date Calculator?

A due date is a useful reference point, but it is not an exact prediction of the day labor will begin. The NHS describes the EDD as an estimate and notes that pregnancy commonly lasts between 37 and 42 weeks from the first day of the last period. NHS due date calculator guidance.

Accuracy can be affected by an uncertain LMP, irregular cycles, variation in ovulation, or uncertainty around conception. A dating ultrasound may be used by a healthcare professional when clinically appropriate.

Pregnancy Trimesters and Key Dates

Tracking your pregnancy week is often more useful than focusing only on the due date. Your result includes trimester information and key dates so you can see where you are in the pregnancy timeline.

  • First trimester: early pregnancy and rapid embryonic development.
  • Second trimester: the middle part of pregnancy, when many routine assessments and developmental milestones occur.
  • Third trimester: the final stage leading toward delivery.

Clinical terminology also distinguishes early term (37+0 to 38+6), full term (39+0 to 40+6), late term (41+0 to 41+6), and postterm (42+0 and beyond). These categories describe gestational age; they do not mean that a birth should be scheduled at a particular week.

Estimated Due Date vs. Actual Birth Date

Your estimated due date should be thought of as a reference point within a broader pregnancy window. It helps organize prenatal care and gives you a useful target for planning, but babies do not all arrive on the same day. If you are approaching or have passed your estimated due date, follow the advice of your maternity care team rather than relying on an online calculator alone.

Sources & Methodology

ClearlyCheck calculates an estimated due date using established pregnancy-dating conventions: LMP-based dating uses 280 days with an adjustment for the entered cycle length; conception dating uses approximately 266 days; IVF dating uses the embryo transfer date and embryo age; ultrasound dating works backward from the gestational age documented at the scan; and the known-due-date option works backward from an established EDD. These calculations are intended for education and planning, not diagnosis or clinical decision-making.

For general pregnancy dating information, see NHS Due Date Calculator guidance and NHS ultrasound guidance. Clinical dating should be confirmed with your doctor, midwife, or other qualified maternity professional.
