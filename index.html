Free calculators for the decisions that actually matter

ClearlyCheck is a small, independent collection of 27 free online calculators covering the everyday money, health, education, and productivity questions most people would rather not do by hand. There's no signup, no paywall, and no account to create — every tool runs entirely in your browser, so the numbers you type never leave your device.

Money & finance

The mortgage calculator breaks a monthly payment down into principal, interest, taxes, insurance, and PMI, then shows the full amortization schedule. The debt-free calculator tells you the exact date your balance hits zero, the compound interest calculator shows how savings grow over time, and the income tax calculator estimates your federal bill for 2025 or 2026. The sales tax calculator adds or removes sales tax from any price. Weighing a big decision? Rent vs buy, can I afford this, and the salary-to-hourly converter turn vague questions into a clear number.

Health & family

The BMI calculator uses the World Health Organization classification, the calorie calculator runs the Mifflin-St Jeor equation dietitians rely on, and the due-date calculator estimates a pregnancy timeline using the same method as most clinics. The height calculator predicts a child's adult height from the parents', the bra size calculator turns two measurements into a band and cup size, and the age calculator works out an exact age down to the day. For the other member of the family, the dog and cat age calculator converts a pet's age into human years, and the pet food and calorie calculator works out daily calories and portions.

Study, work & everyday utility

Students use the GPA calculator and grade calculator to track a target grade; the rest of the toolkit covers a tip and bill splitter, a percentage calculator, a unit converter, a shoe size converter, a timezone meeting planner, a time card calculator for weekly work hours, a date duration calculator, a word count and reading time tool, and a browser-based password generator.

How the tools are kept accurate

Each calculator states the formula it uses, and the money and health tools cite their source — the IRS for tax figures, the CFPB for mortgage terms, the WHO for BMI, and ACOG for pregnancy dating. Every tool is built and reviewed by Enos, the one person behind ClearlyCheck, and pages are dated so you can see when they were last checked. Results are estimates meant to inform your own decisions, not to replace professional advice. Spotted something wrong? Tell us — corrections ship fast.
