What this calculator does

This tool answers two everyday date questions. In Between dates mode it counts how much time separates two dates — as total days, whole weeks, a years-months-days breakdown, and business days only. In Add / subtract mode it takes a starting date and moves it forward or backward by a number of days, weeks, months, or years to land on a new date. Useful for deadlines, notice periods, due dates, contracts, and "how long until…" questions.

Counting the gap: inclusive vs. exclusive

By default the calculator counts the gap between two dates — the number of nights, effectively. From 1 January to 2 January is 1 day. That's what you want for things like interest or age. But for durations where both ends count — a five-day conference, a vacation, a notice period — you want to include the end date, which adds one day. Use the Include the end date toggle to switch between the two, since mixing them up is the single most common date-math mistake.

Total days vs. the years-months-days breakdown

A span can be described two ways, and both are correct. Total days is a straight count — handy for anything you multiply, like daily rates. The years, months, and days breakdown follows the calendar, so it accounts for months of different lengths and for leap years. That's why "3 months" isn't a fixed number of days: from 1 January to 1 April is 90 days, but from 1 July to 1 October is 92.

How business days are counted

Business-day (or working-day) counts include only Monday through Friday and skip weekends. This is what most contracts and shipping estimates mean by "working days." Note that this calculator does not subtract public holidays, because they vary by country, state, and year — if your deadline depends on holidays, remove those days yourself for your location.

Leap years and edge cases

Leap years are handled automatically: February has 29 days in years divisible by four, except century years not divisible by 400. When you add months or years to a date that doesn't exist in the target month — like adding one month to 31 January — the result rolls into the following month, matching how most calendar software behaves. For the exact age of a person from a birth date, the dedicated age calculator is a better fit.

Sources & method

Calculations use the standard Gregorian calendar and the JavaScript date engine in your browser, with leap years applied automatically. Business days count Monday-Friday only and exclude public holidays, which differ by region. Results are for general planning; for legally binding deadlines, confirm how your specific contract or jurisdiction counts days (calendar vs. business days, and whether the first or last day is included). This tool is general guidance, not legal advice.
