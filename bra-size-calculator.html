How to measure for the right size

A bra size is built from two numbers: a band (the number, like 34) and a cup (the letter, like C). You only need two measurements to work them out. For the most reliable result, use a soft tape measure, stand relaxed, and wear a thin, non-padded bra:

  1. Band measurement: wrap the tape snugly around your ribcage, directly underneath your bust. Keep it level and firm but not tight.
  2. Bust measurement: wrap the tape loosely around the fullest part of your chest, usually across the nipple line, keeping it level front and back.

How band and cup are worked out

The band size comes from your underbust measurement, rounded to the nearest even number (band sizes run 28, 30, 32, 34, 36 and so on). The cup size comes from the difference between your bust and band measurements — each inch of difference is roughly one cup:

Notice that US and UK cup letters agree up to a D, then diverge — which is exactly why a size from one brand or country may not match another.

What are sister sizes?

Sister sizes share the same cup volume across different bands. If a band feels too tight, go up one band and down one cup; if it feels too loose, go down one band and up one cup. For example, 34C, 32D, and 36B all hold a similar cup volume. They're handy when your calculated size is sold out or the band doesn't sit right.

Signs a bra doesn't fit

  • Band rides up at the back — the band is too big; try one band smaller.
  • Underwire or cup digs in or overflows — the cup is too small; try one cup up.
  • Gaping cups with room to spare — the cup is too big; try one cup down.
  • Straps dig in and carry all the weight — usually a sign the band is too loose and not doing its job.

Sources & method

This calculator uses the standard band-and-cup method described above: band from the underbust measurement rounded to the nearest even number, cup from the bust-minus-band difference at roughly one inch per cup. There is no single global bra standard — cut and sizing vary between brands and between the US, UK, and European systems — so treat the result as a well-supported starting point, then try the size on along with its sister sizes. This tool is for general guidance and is not medical advice; for fit related to health or post-surgery needs, a professional fitter or clinician is the right call.
