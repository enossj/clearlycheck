How this converter works

For most categories, this converter works by converting your input to a common base unit (meters for length, kilograms for weight, liters for volume, and so on), then converting from that base unit to your target unit. This two-step approach means adding new units only requires knowing their relationship to the base unit, not to every other unit directly.

Why temperature is different

Length, weight, and most other measurements convert by simple multiplication because their scales all start at zero in the same place — zero meters is the same "nothing" as zero feet. Temperature scales don't share a zero point: 0°C (water freezing) is 32°F and 273.15K, three completely different numbers for the same physical temperature. Converting temperature requires an offset calculation (like F = C × 9/5 + 32), not just a multiplier.

Metric vs. imperial: a quick history

The metric system was developed in France in the 1790s, designed around powers of 10 for easy conversion between units. It's now the official system in nearly every country. The imperial system, with roots in medieval English units, persists most prominently in the United States for everyday use — though even the U.S. uses metric extensively in science, medicine, and manufacturing. The UK officially converted to metric decades ago but still commonly uses miles, pounds, and pints in daily life, creating a genuinely mixed system.

Digital storage: decimal vs. binary

This is one of the most common sources of confusion in unit conversion. Storage manufacturers (hard drives, SSDs, USB sticks) use the decimal standard: 1 KB = 1,000 bytes, 1 MB = 1,000 KB, and so on — this is what this converter uses. Operating systems, however, often calculate storage using binary counting: 1 KiB = 1,024 bytes, but display it labeled as "KB." This mismatch is exactly why a drive advertised as 1TB shows up as roughly 931GB when you check it in Windows or macOS — nothing is missing, it's just two different counting conventions being labeled the same way.
