What MPG actually measures

MPG (miles per gallon) tells you how far your vehicle travels on a single gallon of fuel — the higher the number, the more efficient your car is at turning fuel into distance. The most accurate way to find your real-world MPG isn't the sticker on the window at the dealership, but a simple fill-up test: reset your trip odometer when you fill the tank, drive normally until your next fill-up, then divide the miles you drove by the gallons it took to refill. That's exactly what the calculate mode above does for you, and it usually gives a more honest number than the EPA-rated figure since it accounts for your actual driving conditions, terrain, and habits.

US gallons vs. imperial gallons

This calculator uses US gallons (about 3.785 liters), which is the standard fuel measurement in the United States. If you're comparing figures from the UK or another country that uses imperial gallons (about 4.546 liters), be aware that an imperial MPG number looks higher than a US MPG number for the exact same fuel economy, since the imperial gallon holds more fuel. Convert an imperial MPG figure to US gallons before entering it here, or use the L/100km convert mode instead, since liters and kilometers don't have this US/imperial ambiguity.

Why L/100km moves the opposite way

L/100km (liters per 100 kilometers) flips the logic of MPG: instead of asking how far you get on a fixed amount of fuel, it asks how much fuel you burn over a fixed distance. That means a lower L/100km is better, since it means less fuel was needed to cover the same 100 km — the exact opposite of MPG, where a higher number is better. Because the two units describe the same physical quantity from inverse directions, converting between them involves dividing a constant by your value rather than multiplying by a fixed factor, which is why this calculator handles the conversion for you rather than asking you to do the arithmetic by hand.

Sources & method

MPG is calculated as miles driven ÷ gallons used. From that MPG figure, L/100km is calculated as 235.215 ÷ MPG, and km/L is calculated as MPG × 0.425144 — both constants come from the standard US-gallon-to-metric conversion (1 US gallon = 3.785411784 liters, 1 mile = 1.609344 km). Cost per mile, when a fuel price is entered, is simply price per gallon ÷ MPG. This calculator doesn't pull live fuel prices or vehicle data from any feed — all figures come from the numbers you enter.
